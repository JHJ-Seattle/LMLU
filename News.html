Summer 2024 Recap

September 1st, 2024

This summer, Love Me. Love Us. hosted a series of inclusive camps and events that brought joy, creativity, and community to children of all abilities. From art and music workshops to cultural celebrations, our programs provided a safe and welcoming space for kids to express themselves and build friendships.


Sharing A Heartwarming Story From Summer Camp:

Weeks of planning culminated in a moment of brilliance — this is often the feeling among the team members after each event. This year, Love Me Love Us hosted a three-day summer camp for the first time, and the preparation was meticulous. From announcements, registrations, volunteer training, work assignments, project designs, and art materials to snack selections and arrangements, the entire process played out countless times in my mind before the first day of camp finally arrived.

Early in the morning, 20 to 30 volunteers arrived on time; some set up the venue, others managed sign-ins and organized uniforms, and the rest prepared food and tools. The high school volunteers chatted and laughed, occasionally stretched, and exuded confidence and readiness. At that moment, D burst into the classroom with a plastic tube in his mouth, followed by his assigned volunteer, Alex, his mentor, stumbled in after him. D continued to roam, weaving through the children gathered in a circle, as they participated in a music session and the tables filled with canvases and paints. Alex stayed close to D, ensuring he didn't collide with anything or get lost. As time passed, the nearly six-feet-tall Alex started to show signs of fatigue. At the end of the day, I jokingly asked him, "Will you be here tomorrow?" He replied calmly, "I signed up for three days, so I'll be here all three days."

Seeing D again on the second day made me grateful; we were more concerned that his mom might give up. Returning to the same place, D seemed more familiar with the environment and the busy people around. His expression was less tense, and he smiled more. However, his legs didn't stop moving, and he still weaved between the instruments, percussion players, scissors, colored paper, glue, and crayons, as if he was on wheels. He occasionally shouted to express emotions we didn't understand. As promised, Alex showed up and continued being D's bodyguard. For Alex, the second day was still a physical challenge, but he seemed to have gotten used to D's routes and managed more effectively.

On the third day, when I saw D and his mom walk to the sign-in table, it felt like seeing old friends. I expected to see D continue weaving through the music and art activities, but the day turned out to be surprisingly calm. While the kids inside were busy following colorful balloons on a parachute and carefully pasting eyes, mouths, and hair onto their self-portraits, a more exciting story was unfolding outside the classroom. Five high school volunteers were entertaining D, making him laugh, and helping him create his self-portrait with colored paper. Everyone had big smiles on their faces, including D. Finally, the volunteers gathered around D for the cutest group photo ever taken, like a love-written article etched in the diary of Love Me Love Us 2024 summer camp. These warm little stories behind the scenes become the strength in our hearts, continually supporting us through the years, making our path increasingly clearer and firmer

As we look forward to the upcoming fall season, we remain committed to fostering inclusivity and empowerment through our programs. Stay tuned for more exciting events and opportunities to get involved!

Featured in Seattle’s Child Magazine

July 31st, 2024

We are honored to be featured in Seattle’s Child Magazine for our inclusive summer programs that support children with disabilities and their families. As the article highlights, many parents face enormous hurdles finding accessible and affordable care — especially during the summer months.

At Love Me. Love Us., we aim to ease that burden. Through free camps, one-on-one volunteer support, and a welcoming community space for parents, we provide more than childcare — we offer connection, creativity, and a sense of belonging.

“Our idea is having a community to contribute to those with special needs is the realization of social justice.”
– Jingde Hsia, Founder
